<h1 style="font-size:400%;text-align:center;margin-bottom:20px;font-family:Times New Roman;color:black;">The Great MCU Downfall</h1>

<img src="https://i.imgur.com/SQf07CO.png" alt="title" style="float:center;width:1180px;height:250px;">
<h2 style="font-size:100%;font-family:Times New Roman;color:black;">Written by Katrina Ng for DIDA345</h2>
<p>There is something that needs to be talked about that I feel people are noticing but not speaking up about, and that is the <a href="https://time.com/6319815/marvel-cinematic-universe-future/"target="_blank">decline of the Marvel Cinematic Universe</a>. There was a time where the MCU films were holding box office sale records and getting millions of views on trailers for the biggest upcoming movies. You may have heard of these big movies like Avengers Infinity War, Avengers Endgame, Thor, Spiderman: No Way Home, and more. But, why are the newer movies not really talked about? If this franchise is so widely known and popular, where are the youtube videos and TikToks talking about how good they are? Instead, there are more talkings about how the recent films and shows have not been reaching up to their potential. And, because of this short reach to the incredibly huge fanbase, <a href="https://www.forbes.com/sites/carolinereid/2025/09/05/disney-lost-619-million-in-takings-on-each-marvel-movie-over-past-two-years/"target="_blank">box office sales</a> are also declining. On a side note, prices for movie tickets are also always increasing, so would it not make sense for the sales to also increase? This just shows how the decline is hitting harder than anyone would have expected, and here is why. </p>

<h1 style="font-size:330%;text-align:center;margin-bottom:0px;font-family:Times New Roman;color:black;"><span style="color: #645394;">POWER</span> of the MCU</h1>
<p>The Marvel Cinematic Universe is best known for their Avengers team, originally made up of six heroes: Captain America, Iron Man, Hulk, Thor, Black Widow, and Hawkeye. After the franchise’s last big film in 2019 of Avengers: Endgame, half of these original Avengers did not make it to the next movies. Obviously there was sadness and worry from the fans at the time where many did not see how the MCU would recover from the losses of the most popular and loved original characters. There were, of course, other Avengers that would take their place, but no one can beat the original of anything, right? After Avengers: Endgame, the MCU did not fall off completely yet,<a href="https://screenrant.com/marvel-mcu-movies-tv-shows-saved-franchise/"target="_blank"> it was good</a>, as they released following big films like the Spider-Man movies, Shang-Chi, and Black Panther which led fans to believe that there can be new and upcoming favorites to save the ones that were lost. But, that belief soon came to an end. A lot of movies were just horrible. New character and plot upwringings were not doing well at all, like Eternals, The Marvels, and Thunderbolts. Not only that, but movies with loved characters who continued throughout the MCU also did not live up to their standards, such as Ant-Man and the Wasp: Quantumania, Captain America: Brave New World, and Thor: Love and Thunder. People named this era <a href="https://screenrant.com/marvel-tv-shows-not-mcu-canon-reason/"target="_blank">“Post Endgame Crisis,”</a> as the Marvel Cinematic Universe was just struggling to keep their audience engaged after their peak in the industry. Why is this? We have thoughts but we also don’t know. It could be the pressure to release greater movies after their peak, or it could just be the carelessness or complacency of the industry after making it big.</p>
	<p>During the COVID19 pandemic and the rise of <a href="https://pie.yt/?v=https://youtu.be/_k-x2QcMg-o?si=nBWIu0bMDDYS5lPM&pieshare=1"target="_blank"> shows on Disney+</a>, the MCU began to create shows with characters who did not have their own movies or shows that showcased new characters that would eventually be a part of the big Avengers franchise. Because it was difficult to have film production and theatres were locked down due to the pandemic, Disney+ was their idea of continuing to stay engaged with their fanbase and giving us what we want. In the beginning, shows produced were great, getting good reviews, and having multiple seasons. Unfortunately, this trend did not last as I believe the streaming platform took over more of the share of what to produce and what to release and by when, instead of the MCU company itself. Thus, this started the downhill trend once again. In all, MCU shows were the biggest reason for the extreme decline of MCU popularity and image. </p>
	<p>With the initial loss of ideas after Avengers: Endgame and the loss of their beloved characters, the movies and shows were not it. The industry tried too hard to build that engagement again, but I think they tried a little too hard. At this point, the films being released are more duds than wows, as it is clear that they want to release more rather than good. In other words,<a href="https://thetwingeeks.com/2025/07/25/an-ode-to-the-marvel-cinematic-universe-quantity-over-quality/"target="_blank"> quantity over quality</a>. And, because they keep throwing new things at us, it is hard for them to keep up with marketing, that is why a lot of them are lost or never heard of. Also, too many of the shows lack connection with each other, making it harder for fans to follow them, causing them to lose interest very quickly. Overall, a lot of what the industry is handling is very messy. In the beginning Disney+ shows, they released Loki which had a lot of great reviews and the show was really able to show what the MCU is capable of, but in that same week, The Eternals film was released and it is known to be the worst film in the MCU. It is funny how the most popular show and the worst, most criticized movie was released within the same week. You can really tell <a href="https://www.vg247.com/how-loki-season-2-good-when-marvel-bad"target="_blank"> where producers are going wrong and what they are prioritizing</a>, because why is the show with 6 episodes doing substantially better than a whole movie? </p>
	<p>Honestly, there can be so much more to talk about on the broader scale, but let’s look into the smaller details of what is really happening within the Marvel Cinematic Universe Franchise. </p>

<h1 style="font-size:330%;text-align:center;margin-bottom:0px;font-family:Times New Roman;color:black;">The Franchise's <span style="color: #801818;">REALITY</span> </h1>
<img src="https://i.pinimg.com/736x/80/0b/cb/800bcb265eabe35ba398c0ebdb46d8cf.jpg"; style="width:1180px;height:450px;">

<p1>
<a href="https://editorial.rottentomatoes.com/guide/all-marvel-cinematic-universe-movies-ranked/"target="_blank">Rotten tomato scores</a> are the audience's way to show what they thought about films. The higher the better, the more rotten the tomatoes are. Within the MCU, it is seen as phases where after Phase 1, each phase consists of two to three years , not including the pandemic year since no movies or shows were released then. Phase 1-3 are known as the best phases of the MCU, as Phase 1 marks the beginning, the original films of the big industry. Phase 2 marks movies that built the industry’s popularity up to what Phase 3 is, its peak. The peak’s movies did great in terms of both box office sales and rotten tomato scores, showcasing that it was a very good time for the MCU. Phase 4 was when Disney+ began to release their content (as shown by <a href="https://movieweb.com/every-mcu-tv-show-ranked-by-rotten-tomatoes/"target="_blank"> stars with a circle around it</a>) and it was doing pretty well, but it was also the phase where the films were experiencing a great decline. Worst of all during this stage, The Eternals, with a 47% rotten tomato score. The last completed phase, Phase 5 was the worst of the worst for the Marvel Cinematic Universe. The consistency of both the shows and movie reviews are scattered all over the place. The rotten tomato scores are getting lower and lower, and this phase is reaching historical lows. This is very concerning, but as it shows, the one show that was released in Phase 6 has a very high rotten tomato score, so maybe there is hope for this upcoming phase. Let this phase save the previous two. </p1>

<h1 style="font-size:330%;text-align:center;margin-bottom:0px;font-family:Times New Roman;color:black;">Diving Into Our <span style="color: #DECA57;">MIND</span> </h1>
<p>Before diving into detail as to why the newer films are not doing that great, as mentioned earlier there is a lot of recent habit of releasing quantity over quality. I think what is shown above does a great job of showcasing that as there are so many stars and circled stars in Phases 4 and 5 . Phase 4 being the shortest phase and having so many releases during that time. No wonder there is a lack of connection and quality between fans and this franchise. Moving on, to be completely straightforward, Disney+ shows are just not good at all. They are oversaturated and <a href="https://www.reddit.com/r/marvelstudios/comments/1oe5zkw/my_theory_on_why_mcu_disney_shows_failed/"target="_blank">viewers</a> who sit down to watch the shows can tell that producers did not care about what they were producing. Either it is too rushed, too slow, boring, lack of amusement, everything was just very inconsistent. And yes, that is the trend of the shows up until today. Additionally why movies are also being pulled down with the shows, is because the shows are more convoluted. So, without watching the shows, watching some of the upcoming movies will be confusing. Some factors explained from the show come up in the movies, leaving people confused on what they missed out on. Should it not be that to understand some of the shows, you need to watch the movie first? Not the other way around. What if someone does not have Disney+ or other platforms that have access to the shows, will they have to buy a whole subscription just to be all caught up? Or, do they just have to watch the movies in confusion? </p>

<h1 style="font-size:330%;text-align:center;margin-bottom:0px;font-family:Times New Roman;color:black;">What Are Producers Doing With Their <span style="color: #2E8B57;">TIME</span> </h1>
<p>There are so many factors as to why this new MCU system they have going on is extremely unfair, and not only that, but if they wanted to make films more convoluted, at least produce shows that live up to the industry’s image. Because of the lack of these two factors, it can be easily calculated as to why the Marvel Cinematic Universe is on its downfall.</p>

	<p>Before the Disney+ Marvel Cinematic Universe Show’s decline, we must talk about the show that received a very high rotten tomato score (92%) and has the highest number of streams (over 5.23 billion), <a href="https://www.rogerebert.com/streaming/disney-loki-tom-hiddleston-tv-review-2021"target="_blank">Loki</a>. 
    <img src="https://i.pinimg.com/736x/64/bd/16/64bd1660f4808d9806785cc80fcdd41b.jpg" alt="loki" style="float:right;width:325px;height:375px;mix-blend-mode:multiply;">
A paragraph with a floating image. A paragraph with a floating image. A paragraph with a floating image.</p>
	<p>This was one of Disney+ and the MCU’s great ideas. Taking a loved villain and giving him his own show. Having a side character who actually shows potential character growth and already has a big fanbase are the correct factors to input while deciding who to give a show to. When the show aired, people had a lot of good things to say about it, more than the bad. The show was easily able to showcase how much care and thought the directors and producers put into the creation, based on the storyline, the script, camera angles, and more. Of course, there are some things to mention about what could have been better, like the pacing and what the producers decided to include, but those things were not in a whole criticized by everyone. You can’t always please everyone's opinions about things, but Loki having the hearts of many after only the first season was what the MCU needed during Phase 4, their extreme decline. </p>
	<p>Loki was one of only a few of the shows that did well, and even its season 2 was talked highly about, receiving a lot of streams as well. With both seasons’ incorporation of adventure, high stakes, and care into the making of it, no wonder it was extremely successful. This show is a key example as to what the MCU should be doing if they want to release shows that tie into their films. There is no need to watch Loki to understand the future films. And, the end of the season also teases potential new characters and what they could be dealing with, but this does not mean someone NEEDS to watch Loki to know what is going on in that future film. Which is how it should be for all the other shows and movies.</p>

<p>Unfortunately, not all Disney+ tied in MCU shows can be as successful as Loki. Speaking of one of them, <a href="https://www.rogerebert.com/streaming/secret-invasion-tv-review"target="_blank">Secret Invasion</a> is known to be the worst of all. This show revolves around the character Nick Fury, who has been involved in the MCU for a very long time. He is also the one to be involved in all of the original Avengers’ lives since the beginning of their journeys, made small cameos in almost every movie, and is iconic for his eyepatch and ability to weave through the darkness. So, he is quite a popular man around the MCU. But, he also has the worst show to exist so what went wrong? 
<img src="https://i.pinimg.com/736x/a9/da/5f/a9da5f53f96537bea54073a6197d1084.jpg" alt="eye patch" style="float:left;width:400px;height:225px;mix-blend-mode:multiply;"></p>
<p>The bad production, lazy storytelling, and boring plotline might be the reason why. Unlike the care the producers put into Loki, fans are able to tell that the producers really did not care about Nick Fury’s show at all. There were so many talented actors and actresses involved in this show as well, but their talents were wasted. Also, it seemed like Disney+ and the MCU just wanted to get this show to air, because the amount of mistakes the audience was able to notice could have been easily noticed by professionally trained directors. Sadly, there were not many or any good reviews about the show. More reviews on the acting and the vibe of the show, but not anything about the contents of the show. </p>
<p>This goes to show that Disney+ and the MCU crew can take very popular characters and make shows about them, but there is no point in taking those characters if they are not going to be displayed to their full potential. Samuel L. Jackson is a very famous and talented actor who has held his character for Nick Fury very high, up until the point of his show. That is very disappointing. What matters is how much effort people put into the making of the show. A lot of effort put in, like Loki, will result in high success. Low and rushed effort results in shows like Secret Invasion, very unsuccessful. </p>

<h1 style="font-size:330%;text-align:center;margin-bottom:0px;font-family:Times New Roman;color:black;">The <span style="color: #6693F5;">SPACE</span> Between Connections</h1>
<img src="https://i.pinimg.com/736x/57/2f/14/572f14504c216699fba8838a0428abc4.jpg"; style="width:1180px;height:600px;">
<p>In the first three phases of the Marvel Cinematic Universe, the industry did a good job seeding and sprouting the major heroes, as they had a plan for them in the near future. On the other hand, the following phases were not as consistent. In the beginning phases, every hero was returned immediately so that they could be integrated into the bigger Avengers movies. The first phase has the original six avengers, producing the first Avengers movie. The second phase integrated more major heroes either casted in the Avengers movie itself, or came from their own movies, like Ant-Man and Guardians of the Galaxy. The third phase was when the MCU was getting bigger, producing more individual movies before integrating everyone back together for Avengers: Infinity War and Avengers: Endgame. This was an apparent plan the MCU had for its fans. </p>
	<p>Following Phase 3 was when Disney+ started to rise and when all the Phase 1 characters were not really included anymore. As mentioned in the previous pages, this was the start of the decline. This is when fans started to get <a href="https://pie.yt/?v=https://youtu.be/eVImGer3CFc?si=VMt8gmg-PtXurQPx&pieshare=1"target="_blank">lost and confused</a> as to what the MCU’s next big picture was going to be. There seems to have been no big picture, since Phase 4, Phase 4 and 5 have not returned back into the franchise. In comparison to the first three phases, major heroes were integrated back in almost immediately. Yet, it is taking years, at most 4 so far for these heroes to return to the screen or even be mentioned. And also, why are the movie current characters being revisited sooner than the ones fans have been waiting on for years. I personally remember being excited for Shang-Chi’s next appearance after watching it for the first time, but now I do not even remember what the first movie was about because it’s been so long. Not only are characters being introduced and not kept, each movie and show gives the audience a thread that can be followed throughout the MCU. For example, how the first three phases had all the six infinity stones scattered throughout different movies, for them to finally come together during Avengers: Infinity War. Now, there are mentions about certain characters of timelines that were never mentioned ever again. So, what was the point of mentioning them in the first place? Like, why mention The White Vision and how he has been missing for 5 years in the Disney+ show WandaVision, released in 2021, if his character is never actually going to come up. <a href="https://time.com/6319815/marvel-cinematic-universe-future/"target="_blank">They created a character and did not know how to use it</a>. Or, ending a movie with a giant stone god in the ocean that could have potentially changed a bunch of future projects, but then never expanded on it. There are so many things the MCU has been doing that leaves fans in a “what now?” thought. </p>
	<p>Speaking of how the timelines and stories of Phase 4 and 5 heroes are not aligning, I do not understand why some of their new major heroes got their own shows or films to begin with. Unlike Loki and Nick Fury from Secret Invasion, the new shows featuring She-Hulk or the new Captain America are apparent <a href="https://time.com/6319815/marvel-cinematic-universe-future/"target="_blank">B or C list characters</a> who do not need their own show or movie. Respectfully, their characters were okay where they were to begin with. <a href="https://screenrant.com/mcu-marvel-movies-phases-future-plan-feige-myth/"target="_blank">What is the MCU’s plan and future now?</a> There is not much to look forward to anymore, no connections, no possible favorite heroes coming together. Where are the big movies where everyone is there? The way the Marvel Cinematic Universe was working really well in the past, there was no reason to change how things were going. </p>

<h1 style="font-size:330%;text-align:center;margin-bottom:0px;font-family:Times New Roman;color:black;">Future <span style="color: #F4AB6A;">SOUL</span> of the MCU</h1>
<p>	There have been new producers and directors for the MCU after the Warner’s Brothers retired, but these producers should be careful of what they are doing. If this trend of quantity over quality or heroes with dead ends continue throughout the next phases, the Marvel Cinematic Universe will continue to receive criticism and lose their audience. This loss of connectivity between the characters of the films also loses the connectivity between the films and the fans. Without the fans, this huge franchise will be no more. In the future, I hope to see the phases <a href="https://variety.com/2025/film/news/marvel-kevin-feige-robert-downey-jr-miles-morales-1236465488/"target="_blank">rebuilding their shape again</a>, favorite heroes coming back in a timely manner, seeing characters being portrayed up to their worth, and the Marvel Cinematic Universe booming again! Phase 6 better be known as the ultimate comeback!</p>
